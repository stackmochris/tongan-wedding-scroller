# Audio-Visual Media in Language Learning

## Meta-Analytic Evidence

Sutton et al. meta-analysis (Studies in Second Language Acquisition; 75 effect sizes from 56 experiments, n≈1,954):

- Overall medium effect of audiovisual input on L2 learning (g ≈ 0.89–1.01 depending on exact report).
- Benefits observed across vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation, listening, and speaking.
- Educational / language-focused video (documentaries, TED-style, targeted lessons) produced stronger effects than pure entertainment video.
- Captions (L2 on-screen text) are generally beneficial, especially for vocabulary and listening.

## Mechanisms

- Multimodal input provides richer context and supports comprehension.
- Authentic audiovisual material exposes learners to natural speech rates, accents, and cultural framing.
- On-screen text (captions) aids form-meaning mapping and vocabulary noticing.
- Gains are typically modest-to-medium; AV is a strong supplement, not a complete replacement for other input and practice.

## Practical Considerations

- Prefer content that is mostly comprehensible (i+1) rather than fully native-speed unassisted material for beginners.
- Captions in the target language are usually preferable to L1 subtitles for acquisition once basic comprehension is possible.
- Interest and cultural relevance increase sustained exposure.
- Combine with retrieval practice for better retention of new items encountered in video.

## Implications for Design

- Integrate short, level-appropriate video or audio with captions.
- Pair media exposure with immediate or spaced retrieval tasks.
- Prioritise educational or carefully scaffolded content over unfiltered entertainment for core learning pathways.
- Cultural stories and domain-specific media (health, daily life) align well with Polynesian language goals.
- Portable: same principles apply; source or create language-specific audiovisual materials.

**Sources**: Sutton et al. meta-analysis (Cambridge / Studies in Second Language Acquisition); Montero Perez review on audiovisual input and on-screen text; related captioning and incidental vocabulary studies.
