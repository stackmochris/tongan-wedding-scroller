# Proven Learning Techniques and Systems

## Core Evidence-Based Principles

No single method is universally best. Reliable principles include:

1. **Comprehensible input (i+1)** — Understanding messages slightly above current level is necessary (Krashen; accepted as necessary even by critics).
2. **Spaced retrieval / testing effect** — Retrieving information strengthens memory more than re-reading (Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Bjork on desirable difficulties).
3. **Learning in meaningful context / whole sentences** — Stronger traces than isolated pairs.
4. **Balanced curriculum** — Nation’s four strands: meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output, language-focused learning, fluency development (approximately equal time).

## Vocabulary-Specific Findings

Paul Nation’s work (e.g., *Learning Vocabulary in Another Language*; four strands papers):

- High-frequency vocabulary (roughly first 2,000–3,000 word families) provides high coverage for basic communication.
- Both quantity of meetings and quality of processing matter.
- Explicit, deliberate study of vocabulary is effective and durable; it complements incidental learning from input.
- Extensive reading is highly efficient for vocabulary growth (Nation; Jeon & Day 2016 meta-analysis on extensive reading).

## Output

Merrill Swain’s Output Hypothesis: producing language can facilitate learning via:
- Noticing gaps
- Hypothesis testing
- Metalinguistic reflection
- Fluency development

Input remains primary; output is complementary under conditions that push learners to notice and refine form.

## Practical Systems

- Spaced-repetition systems (Anki-style) for vocabulary retention.
- Extensive reading / listening of graded or interest-based material.
- Task-based interaction with corrective feedback (strong evidence in instructed SLA).
- Combination of the above outperforms single-method approaches in controlled studies (e.g., multidomain spacing + retrieval studies showing large gains).

## Implications for Design

- Provide abundant, level-appropriate input (stories, dialogues, domain content).
- Build in retrieval practice (quizzes, flashcards, spaced review).
- Include opportunities for output (speaking, writing, matching tasks).
- Structure content so high-frequency and high-utility terms are prioritised early.
- Portable: techniques are language-agnostic; adapt frequency lists and cultural content per language.

**Sources**: Krashen; Nation (four strands, vocabulary research); Swain; Roediger & Karpicke (2006); Cepeda et al. (2006); Kim & Webb (2022) meta-analysis on spacing; extensive reading meta-analyses.
