# Language Learning in Children

## Critical / Sensitive Periods

Large-scale evidence shows grammar-learning ability remains strong until approximately age 17–18, then declines. Ultimate native-like attainment is strongly associated with earlier start (by ~age 10 for near-native grammar).

- Hartshorne, Tenenbaum & Pinker (2018). A critical period for second language acquisition: Evidence from 2/3 million English speakers. *Cognition*. Dataset of 669,498 participants. Grammar-learning ability preserved until ~17.4 years, then declines. Starting by age 10 predicts higher ultimate attainment.
- ACTFL Research Brief: The Impact of Early Language Learning. Notes that younger learners have an ultimate-attainment advantage with ample naturalistic input, while older children/adults often show faster initial progress in constrained classroom time.

## Input Quantity and Quality

Children from language-rich environments show substantially larger vocabularies. Hart & Risley-style findings (cited in revitalisation literature) link daily sentence exposure to vocabulary growth.

- Language acquisition and language revitalisation literature notes that talkative families produce children with roughly double the vocabulary of less talkative families by 30 months, with the gap continuing to widen (summarised from Hart & Risley data).

## First-Language Mechanisms

Child first-language grammar correlates with both declarative and procedural memory systems (Hamrick, Lum & Ullman 2018 meta-analyses). Lexical knowledge is more strongly tied to declarative memory.

## Implications for Design

- High-volume, rich, comprehensible input in early years supports long-term outcomes.
- Naturalistic or near-naturalistic exposure (stories, songs, family interaction) aligns with evidence for early learners.
- Portable to other Polynesian languages: same age-related patterns apply; cultural relevance of input becomes the variable.

**Sources**: Hartshorne et al. (2018); ACTFL; Hamrick, Lum & Ullman (2018); revitalisation synthesis citing Hart & Risley.
