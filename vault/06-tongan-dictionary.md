# English–Tongan Dictionary Reference

## Canonical Source

**C. Maxwell Churchward (1959). *Tongan Dictionary: Tongan-English and English-Tongan*.** London: Oxford University Press. Approximately 836 pages.

- Widely regarded as the definitive mid-20th-century bilingual dictionary.
- Contains tens of thousands of entries (modern digital transcriptions cite ~37,000+ entries and 40,000+ definitions).
- Includes both directions and extensive cross-references.
- Available via Internet Archive (borrow/stream) and library collections; not freely redistributable in full text without rights clearance.

## Digital Status

- Commercial apps (iOS/Android) offer hand-transcribed versions of Churchward with bidirectional search, offline access, and ongoing corrections. These are paid products.
- No large-scale, freely licensed open-source full dictionary equivalent to Churchward is widely available for unrestricted reuse.
- Smaller or specialised glossaries exist (medical, educational, community resources) but do not replace the comprehensive reference.

## Other Resources

- Edgar Tuʻinukuafe, *A Simplified Dictionary of Modern Tongan* (1992, Polynesian Press) — more accessible modern compilation with pronunciation and grammar notes.
- Community and domain-specific glossaries (e.g., medical terminology projects supported by language funds) provide practical coverage in targeted areas.

## Implications for Product Development

- Churchward remains the scholarly gold-standard reference for accuracy and coverage.
- For an open or community tool, start with carefully licensed or original domain glossaries (health, education, everyday, SDG, etc.) rather than attempting full unrestricted reproduction of Churchward.
- Bidirectional search, proper handling of Tongan orthography (ng as single letter, fakau'a for the glottal stop — note: “ʻokina” is the Hawaiian term), and offline capability are expected features based on existing digital implementations.
- Portability: each target language (Samoan, Niuean, Hawaiian, Tokelauan, Fijian, Reo Māori) will need its own authoritative dictionary sources; the same modular glossary architecture can be reused.
