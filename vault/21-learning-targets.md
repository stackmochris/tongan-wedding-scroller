# Learning Targets per Beat

Each beat states what the player can do by the end, the core phrases practised, the learning principle it serves, and a short sequence-end revision check. All targets stay inside Beginner+ and use only high-utility items introduced in context.

Principles referenced: comprehensible input (Krashen), spaced retrieval (Roediger & Karpicke; Cepeda et al.), meaningful context, output for noticing (Swain), high-frequency vocabulary priority (Nation).

## 1. Airport & Identity
**By the end the player can** greet and state who they are.
**Core phrases**: Malo e lelei / Ko hai? / Ko au… / Ko e… / Fēfē hake?
**Principle**: Comprehensible input + first output. Identity language is high-frequency and immediately useful.
**Revision check**: Greeter asks “Ko hai?” Player must select the correct self-introduction to proceed. Incorrect choice triggers a slowed repeat with no new vocabulary.

## 2. Car Ride — Pointing
**By the end the player can** point out and name simple landmarks.
**Core phrases**: Sio… / Ko ena… / Ko e… / Ki muʻa / ʻOku…
**Principle**: Meaningful visual context. Input is tied to what is on screen; no translation on first exposure.
**Revision check**: Driver points at one landmark already seen. Player selects the matching “Ko ena…” line. Wrong choice simply re-points.

## 3. First Family Dinner
**By the end the player can** use family role terms and make or respond to a basic food offer.
**Core phrases**: Kai / ʻOku ou fie… / Malo / Fāmili / Kui / Tuongaʻane / Tuofefine + 3–4 basic food names
**Principle**: High-frequency vocabulary in a repeated social routine. Output is required to accept or offer food.
**Revision check**: Elder offers food. Player must choose a polite accept or decline that re-uses “Malo” or “ʻOku ou fie…”. Incorrect choice is met with a gentle re-offer.

## 4a. Market / Supplies
**By the end the player can** request an item and confirm a quantity.
**Core phrases**: ʻOku ou fiemaʻu… / Fīha? / Mahu / ʻIkai / Malo
**Principle**: Task-based output. The request must be produced to obtain the item (Swain noticing).
**Revision check**: Shopkeeper asks what is needed. Player selects the correct request. Wrong choice is redirected without new words.

## 4b. Church Check
**By the end the player can** use a polite address and confirm a time.
**Core phrases**: Faifekau / Lotu / Taimi / Malo e tokoni / ʻOku mau…
**Principle**: Register shift. Same core thanks language appears in a more formal setting (spaced retrieval).
**Revision check**: Minister asks for confirmation of the time. Player must select the polite confirmation. Incorrect choice receives a slower repeat.

## 4c. Sports / Youth
**By the end the player can** use basic motion verbs and invite or respond to an invitation.
**Core phrases**: Vaʻinga / Haʻu / ʻAlu / Tau… / Lelei
**Principle**: Fluency strand. Lower formality, higher repetition of everyday verbs already glimpsed.
**Revision check**: Cousin says “Haʻu.” Player selects the matching accept or a simple “Lelei.” Wrong choice is laughed off and repeated.

## 5. Wedding Eve Preparation
**By the end the player can** produce the opening thanks line of the required contribution.
**Core phrases**: Malo e lelei kia moutolu / ʻOku ou fakamālō… / Fāmili / ʻOfa (plus role-specific fragments)
**Principle**: Retrieval of previously successful phrases immediately before the high-stakes use.
**Revision check**: Supportive relative prompts the first thanks line. Player must select it correctly to mark the speech or dance as rehearsed.

## 6. Wedding Climax
**By the end the player can** deliver the short formal contribution in the correct order (speech) or sequence (tauʻolunga).
**Core phrases**: All prior ceremonial and role-specific items, now chained.
**Principle**: Desirable difficulty + output under mild pressure. Success requires retrieving the practised order, not new language.
**Revision check**: The climax itself. Three incorrect choices or insufficient joiners restart only the sequence, forcing another retrieval attempt.

## 7. Ending / Return
**By the end the player can** give and receive a short farewell.
**Core phrases**: ʻAlu ā / Nofo ā / Te u toe haʻu / ʻOfa atu
**Principle**: Final spaced retrieval of high-utility social language in a new but simple context.
**Revision check**: Cousin or elder offers a farewell. Player selects the matching leave-taking line to board transport.

## Notes
- Every revision check re-uses only language already successfully chosen earlier in the same beat or a previous beat.
- No new vocabulary is introduced inside a revision check.
- Role-specific targets (speech structure or dance pairs) are added inside beats 4 and 5 for the relevant path only.
- All cultural phrasing remains subject to DR L.V.S confirmation.
