# Second Language Learning in Adults

## Age Effects Are Real but Not Absolute

Adults are not incapable of high proficiency. The Hartshorne et al. (2018) analysis shows the sharp drop in grammar-learning trajectory occurs around 17–18, not in early childhood. Adults retain capacity for substantial gains.

- Emergentist accounts emphasise changing environmental supports, cognitive abilities, and motivation across the lifespan rather than a hard genetic cutoff (e.g., discussions in Long and related SLA literature).
- Adults often make faster initial progress in instructed settings because of greater cognitive resources and metalinguistic awareness (ACTFL brief; Muñoz 2006 BAF project).

## Memory Systems

Adult L2 grammar at lower proficiency is more strongly linked to declarative memory; at higher proficiency it shifts toward procedural memory (Hamrick, Lum & Ullman 2018 meta-analyses).

- Lexical abilities remain primarily declarative across stages.
- Techniques that support declarative learning (spaced retrieval, explicit attention) are especially useful early; proceduralisation supports fluency later.

## Advantages and Constraints

**Advantages**:
- Stronger analytical skills and explicit learning capacity.
- Ability to use deliberate study strategies effectively.

**Constraints**:
- Reduced opportunity for massive naturalistic input compared with children.
- Motivation and time compete with work/family demands.
- Ultimate native-like phonological and grammatical attainment is rarer after the sensitive period, though high functional proficiency is achievable.

## Implications for Design

- Combine comprehensible input with deliberate, spaced practice.
- Support explicit noticing (especially early) while providing abundant meaningful input.
- Design for intermittent use and clear progress signals to maintain adult motivation.
- Portable across Polynesian languages: cognitive mechanisms are general; content and cultural framing change.

**Sources**: Hartshorne et al. (2018); Hamrick, Lum & Ullman (2018); ACTFL; Long on maturational constraints; emergentist syntheses.
